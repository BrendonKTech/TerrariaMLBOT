TerrariaMLBot Research Log

A bot learning to play Terraria from scratch with reinforcement learning. It started in a Journey mode world and moved to classic worlds in run 3. It gets points for good outcomes and loses points for bad ones, and slowly works out what to do. This log records each change we made to the bot and what its training graphs did afterwards.

Last updated Oct 30, 2026, 8:15 AM EDT · 3 training runs · 1.12M decisions trained in total

How it works

The setup

A small Terraria mod acts as the bot's eyes and hands. Fifteen times a second it pauses the game, sends Python a snapshot of what the player can see, and waits for the bot to choose what to press. The bot is a small neural network trained with PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization, a standard reinforcement learning method). It tries things, sees which choices led to points, and shifts toward doing more of those.

Changelog

What we changed, and what happened

  1. Oct 294:10 – 7:43 PM

    BuildVersion 1 of the bot

    Built the mod (eyes and hands) and the Python trainer (brain), as described in the setup above. Episodes started wherever the player happened to be, and the item reward counted how many items were in the inventory.

  2. Oct 298:00 – 9:47 PM

    Run 1First training run

    Trained for 84k decisions across four sessions. The bot started out dying constantly: its average life fell as low as 42 seconds. It then improved steadily, reaching 127 seconds by the end, and its reward climbed from a low of −4.7 to +5.4. Its choices went from fully random to 93% random, so it was starting to form habits.

    Average life 42 s → 127 sReward −4.7 → +5.4

    A code review afterwards found that two skills were out of reach in this version. The cursor sat about 6 tiles away, beyond pickaxe range, so the bot could not mine or place blocks. And the item reward could be farmed by placing a block and mining it back. Run 1 mostly learned to move and fight.

  3. Oct 299:50 – 10:14 PM

    ChangeFixes from the review

    • Aim distance. A new choice: aim at the tile next to the player, 3.5 tiles out, or far. The first two are within pickaxe and block reach, so mining and building became possible.
    • Closed reward loopholes. Hits on target dummies (which never die), critters, statue spawns and friendly NPCs no longer count. The item reward now pays once for each new kind of item per episode instead of counting stacks.
    • Consistent episode starts. Each episode now begins at the spawn point at dawn, with full health, the starting inventory and nearby enemies cleared. When every start looks the same, the bot can tell good choices apart from lucky circumstances. The mod also lets training turn enemy spawns up or down.
    • Separate ports. Each game copy gets its own connection, so you can watch the bot play in one copy while it trains in another.

    The brain was started from scratch afterwards, because the new aim choice changed the set of available actions.

  4. Oct 2910:16 – 10:50 PM

    Run 2Training with the fixes

    Trained for 32k decisions. It has not died once: all of its roughly ten episodes lasted the full 200 seconds. Reward rose from +4.4 to +7.0. Its choices are still 95% random, which is normal this early. Run 1 was at about the same level at the same point.

    Average life 200 s, no deathsReward +4.4 → +7.0

    Part of this survival comes from easier starts: dawn, at spawn, with enemies cleared. That is the purpose of an early training stage. Night starts and higher spawn rates are the next difficulty steps. Ten episodes is also a small sample.

  5. Oct 29~10:55 PM

    ChangeBetter measurement

    • Reward breakdown. Each episode's reward is now split into kills, damage dealt, damage taken, exploration, new items and deaths, and graphed separately. That shows which skills the bot is actually learning, not just whether the total went up.
    • One graph per run. Each new brain gets its own TensorBoard graph, so runs no longer draw over each other.
  6. Oct 2911:01 PM

    ChangeTraining in several games at once

    The game runs at a fixed 60 updates per second, so one copy produces about 15 decisions per second at most. Training can now use several Terraria copies at once, each on its own port, with one shared brain learning from all of them. This PC (32 GB RAM, 8-core CPU) can run about four copies, which should give roughly 4× the experience per hour: 1M decisions in about 5 hours instead of 20.

    First used in run 3, below.

  7. Oct 29–3011:18 PM – 4:16 AM

    Run 3Overnight, four classic games at once

    Switched from Journey mode to four new classic characters on four small classic worlds. Enemies hit much harder there, and deaths have consequences. The brain carried on from run 2. With four games feeding one brain it trained at 55 decisions per second, about 3.5× one game, and finished 1M decisions in just under five hours.

    It got worse. In the first half hour it lived about 154 seconds per episode. By 12:23 AM that was under 100 seconds, and it ended at 76. Reward fell from +2.9 to −2.9 (first half hour against last). It nearly stopped fighting: kills fell from about 1.1 to 0.1 per episode. It also picked up fewer new items, but explored slightly more. Its choices went from 93% to 81% random, so it was settling into those habits.

    Average life 200 s → 76 sReward +7.0 → −3.0Kills per episode 1.1 → 0.1

    Probable cause: in classic mode every death leaves a tombstone. Hundreds of deaths near spawn likely piled up enough of them to turn spawn into a Graveyard biome, where zombies spawn even in daytime. The in-game chat showed mostly zombie deaths, even though every episode started at dawn. If so, the run made its own starting area harder as it went, which fits life getting shorter all night. Nothing rewarded staying alive either, so dying early cost only the −5 penalty.

  8. Oct 30~8:00 AM

    ChangeFixes after run 3

    • Tombstones removed on every reset. The world stays the same no matter how many times the bot has died in it, so a Graveyard can't build up.
    • Reward for staying alive. +0.002 per decision, about +6 for a full 200-second episode. A long careful life now beats a short reckless one.
    • Cause of death is recorded. The mod reports what killed the bot ("Zombie", "fell to their death", ...), and TensorBoard graphs deaths per cause. Problems now show up in the numbers instead of only in the chat log.

    The next run starts with a new brain, because run 3's brain had settled into the reckless habit.

Plan

What comes next

Pure trial and error is good at moment-to-moment control: moving, fighting, dodging, mining. It is poor at long chains like "gather wood, build a workbench, craft a table, build a room". So the plan mixes the two. The bot learns the control skills, and the mod handles a few long multi-step jobs for it. New abilities are unlocked one stage at a time, so the bot isn't overwhelmed with options it can't use yet.

  1. Item awareness. The bot sees what each hotbar item does, and picks items by purpose (weapon, pickaxe, block, torch, potion) instead of slot number.
  2. Mining stage. Reward for digging down and finding ore.
  3. Crafting. A "craft this" action for about 30 early-game recipes, offered only when the recipe is actually possible.
  4. Building a house. First as an action the mod carries out once the bot has gathered the materials, so NPCs can move in.
  5. First bosses. King Slime and the Eye of Cthulhu, trained in their own stage with a fixed set of gear.
  6. Progression manager. A checklist that picks the next goal and tells the learned skills what to do.
Reading the graphs

Terms used here

Decision (step)
One choice of buttons. The bot makes 15 per second of game time.
Episode
One attempt, ending at death or after 3,000 decisions (200 seconds). Then it starts again.
Reward
The bot's score for an episode, added up from the points listed in the setup. TensorBoard calls it ep_rew_mean.
Average life
Average episode length, converted to seconds. TensorBoard calls it ep_len_mean.
Randomness
How spread out the bot's choices are, from 100% (pure guessing) down toward 0% (always the same choice in the same situation). It should fall slowly. If it falls too fast, the bot stops trying new things.
Rolling average
TensorBoard averages the last 100 episodes, so early points cover only a few episodes and jump around more.
